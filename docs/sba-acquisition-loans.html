SBA loans used to buy a business, by industry

SBA's loan data marks some loans with a business age of "Change of Ownership", meaning the borrower bought an existing business. Here's how those loans break down.

Takeaways

  • Acquisition loans are much larger than the typical 7(a) loan. Since FY2020 the median change-of-ownership loan is $700,000, vs $200,000 for all 7(a) loans.
  • Car-wash acquisitions are the biggest of these industries (median $1,227,450), likely because of land and equipment. That's our inference, not something the data states.

How we calculated this

  • Source: SBA 7(a) FOIA loan-level files, data as of June 30, 2026.
  • Loans counted: every 7(a) loan with that NAICS code, excluding cancelled and undisbursed loans.
  • Charge-off rate (count) = loans with status "Charged Off" ÷ loans counted. Charge-off $ rate = total gross charge-off amount ÷ total gross approved amount.
  • SBA withholds the status of loans that are still active (FOIA Exemption 4), so younger cohorts look better than they will end up. That's why FY2010-2019 is our main cohort.
