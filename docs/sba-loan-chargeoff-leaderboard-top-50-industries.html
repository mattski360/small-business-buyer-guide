SBA 7(a) loans approved FY2010-2019 · data as of June 30, 2026 · 241,584 loans in these 50 industries out of 478,963 total

Which kinds of small businesses most often failed to repay an SBA 7(a) loan? We took every 7(a) loan approved in fiscal 2010 through 2019, kept the 50 six-digit NAICS industries with the most loans, and ranked them by the share that ended up charged off (written off as a loss). Most of these loans have now either been paid off or charged off, so this cohort gives a fairly complete picture.

All 7(a) loans in this cohort: 7.1% charged off by count, 2.9% of approved dollars charged off, median loan $125,000. 28 of the 50 industries came in below the all-loan count rate.

What it shows

  • Lowest: Veterinary Services (2.0%), followed by hotels (except casino hotels) and motels (2.4%) and offices of dentists (2.6%). Highest: residential remodelers (11.9%), then fitness and recreational sports centers (10.1%) and janitorial services (10.0%).
  • Our read (inference, not something the data proves): the low end is crowded with licensed professional practices (vets, dentists, physicians) and real-estate-heavy businesses (hotels, gas stations, child care centers), where loans tend to be larger and backed by property or a licensed owner. The high end is mostly lower-barrier businesses: residential remodelers and home builders, gyms, janitorial services, grocery and sporting-goods retail, drycleaners and restaurants.
  • Count and dollar rates can rank differently. Remodelers and other small-loan trades lose a larger share of dollars; hotels and gas stations have big loans but rarely default.
  • This covers every use of loan proceeds (startups, expansions, real estate, refinancing and acquisitions), not just business purchases, and it reflects 2010s economic conditions.

Caveats

  • NAICS codes in the file follow the version in use when each loan was made. Full-service and limited-service restaurants appear under both the older 2007 codes (722110, 722211) and the newer ones (722511, 722513), so restaurants show up twice. We did not merge them.
  • 2 loans in the cohort had no NAICS code and are left out.
  • Industries with more withheld (still-active) loans, such as hotels (21% withheld), have more outcomes still pending than industries whose loans have mostly closed out.

How we calculated this

  • Source: SBA 7(a) FOIA file for FY2010-FY2019, data as of June 30, 2026.
  • Top 50 = the 50 NAICS codes with the most counted loans; industry names are SBA's NaicsDescription field.
  • Loans counted: every loan in the file for that group, excluding cancelled, undisbursed ("COMMIT") and not-funded loans.
  • Charge-off rate (count) = loans with status "Charged Off" ÷ loans counted. Charge-off $ rate = total gross charge-off amount ÷ total gross approved amount.
  • SBA withholds the status of loans that are still active (shown as "EXEMPT" in the file), so a loan that hasn't charged off yet counts as not charged off. Groups with more withheld loans may see their rates rise.
