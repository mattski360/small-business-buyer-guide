SBA 504 loans by industry: sizes and outcomes

SBA 504 loans approved FY2010 to June 2026 · data as of June 30, 2026

The SBA 504 program finances owner-occupied real estate and major equipment. A typical project is split three ways: a bank first mortgage, a second loan from a Certified Development Company (CDC, the SBA-backed piece), and the buyer's own cash. Buying a business that owns its building, or buying the building along with the business, is where 504 comes into play.

Read this as a maturity effect, not a trend in loan quality: 504 loans run 20-25 years, so most loans from recent years are still active and their status is withheld.

What it shows

  • 504 loans were charged off far less often than 7(a) loans. Among the top 25 504 industries in FY2010-2019, the 504 rate was lower than the same industry's 7(a) rate in 24 of 25. Dentists (0.3%), self-storage, insurance agencies and engineering firms were among the lowest.
  • Our inference: 504 loans are secured by real estate or long-lived equipment and sit behind a bank first mortgage plus the owner's equity, so they should hold up better than general-purpose 7(a) loans. Long terms also mean many FY2010s loans are still active, so these rates may still rise.
  • Hotels lead on size: median CDC loan $1,362,000 (FY2010-2019) and $1,881,500 since FY2020.
  • Buyouts are a small slice of 504: only 3.0% of FY2020+ 504 loans were flagged "Change of Ownership," highest in hotels, gas stations and car washes. Most business purchases are financed with 7(a) (see acquisition loans).

How we calculated this

  • Source: SBA 504 FOIA file, FY2010 to present, data as of June 30, 2026. The 504 amount (GrossApproval) is the CDC portion only.
  • Gas stations: the newer 2022 NAICS code 457110 is merged into 447110.
  • Loans counted: every loan in the file for that group, excluding cancelled, undisbursed ("COMMIT") and not-funded loans.
  • Charge-off rate (count) = loans with status "Charged Off" ÷ loans counted. Charge-off $ rate = total gross charge-off amount ÷ total gross approved amount.
  • SBA withholds the status of loans that are still active (shown as "EXEMPT" in the file), so a loan that hasn't charged off yet counts as not charged off. Groups with more withheld loans may see their rates rise.
