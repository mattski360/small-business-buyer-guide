Utah

SBA 7(a) loans with a Utah project location in this industry: 252 in FY2010-2019 (median $140,250) and 92 from FY2020 to June 2026 (median $350,000). SBA 504 loans in Utah: 95 and 56. Small counts, so treat the Utah medians loosely.

What it shows

  • Dental loans held up far better than average: 2.6% charged off by count in FY2010-2019 vs 7.1% for all 7(a) loans, and only 1.2% of dollars.
  • Loans are large. The FY2020+ median 7(a) loan was $553,000, and the median change-of-ownership loan was $767,500.
  • Practice purchases were 9.1% of FY2020+ dental 7(a) loans. Startups were a bigger group (623 loans flagged "Startup").
  • Utah payroll per employee ($42,383) was well below the U.S. figure ($60,718) in 2023. This data doesn't say why (staff mix, part-time share, and how owner-dentists are paid could all play a part), so don't read it as a wage benchmark.
  • Franchise loans are rare here (0.2% since FY2020).

How we calculated this

  • SBA 7(a) and 504 FOIA loan-level files (as of June 30, 2026), filtered to NAICS 621210. Cancelled, undisbursed ("COMMIT") and 504 "NOT FUNDED" loans are excluded.
  • Charge-off rate (count) = loans with status "Charged Off" ÷ loans counted. Charge-off $ rate = gross charge-off amount ÷ gross approved amount.
  • Acquisition loans = loans whose BusinessAge field is "Change of Ownership". Franchise share = loans with any FranchiseCode.
  • Census figures are from the 2023 County Business Patterns national (cbp23us) and state (cbp23st) files, all legal forms of organization.
  • Covers every use of loan funds, not only purchases, unless labeled. Past loan outcomes don't predict any single deal.
