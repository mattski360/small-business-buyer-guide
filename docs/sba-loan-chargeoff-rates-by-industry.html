Which industries hold up? SBA 7(a) charge-off rates by industry

Out of hundreds of thousands of SBA 7(a) loans made from fiscal 2010 to 2019, what share were eventually charged off (written off as a loss)? It's one rough, public signal of how businesses in an industry hold up under debt.

What it shows

  • In this cohort, laundromats/drycleaners (4.1%) and car washes (5.0%) were charged off less often than all 7(a) loans (7.1%), and well below limited-service restaurants (9.4%).
  • HVAC/plumbing and landscaping came in at 6.4% and 6.4% by count. Their dollar loss rates were higher than car washes or laundries, partly because those loans are smaller and shorter (see loan sizes & terms).
  • These numbers cover every use of loan funds (startups, expansions, acquisitions, real estate), not just business purchases. For purchases, see acquisition loans.

How we calculated this

  • Source: SBA 7(a) FOIA loan-level files, data as of June 30, 2026.
  • Loans counted: every 7(a) loan with that NAICS code, excluding cancelled and undisbursed loans.
  • Charge-off rate (count) = loans with status "Charged Off" ÷ loans counted. Charge-off $ rate = total gross charge-off amount ÷ total gross approved amount.
  • SBA withholds the status of loans that are still active (FOIA Exemption 4), so younger cohorts look better than they will end up. That's why FY2010-2019 is our main cohort.
