How to evaluate a small business for sale

A working checklist, roughly in the order you'll need it. Bring in a CPA and an attorney for the deal itself.

1. First screen (before an NDA)

  • Asking price vs stated cash flow: compare to published multiples.
  • Industry track record: see SBA loan outcomes by industry.
  • Why is the owner selling, and why now?
  • Is the location, license, or lease transferable?

2. Earnings quality

  • 3 years of tax returns, P&Ls and balance sheets, and whether they agree.
  • Every add-back to cash flow, documented and defensible.
  • Bank deposits reconciled to reported revenue.
  • Trend: growing, flat, or declining, and why.

3. Customers and revenue

  • Customer concentration: share of revenue from the top 1, 5 and 10 customers.
  • Recurring vs one-time revenue; contracts and whether they transfer.
  • Seasonality.

4. Owner dependence and people

  • What does the owner personally do each week? Who else can do it?
  • Key employees: tenure, pay vs market, likelihood they stay.
  • Will the seller agree to a transition period?

5. Assets and operations

  • Equipment age, condition, and replacement cost.
  • Lease terms remaining, renewal options, rent vs market.
  • Working capital and inventory needed at close.

6. Financing fit

  • Does cash flow cover debt payments with room to spare after paying yourself?
  • How much cash you'll put in, and whether the seller will carry a note.
  • What similar deals borrowed: see SBA acquisition loans by industry.

7. Before you close

  • Letter of intent terms, then a due diligence period.
  • Professional review of the financials (CPA) and purchase agreement (attorney).
  • Insurance, licenses and permits for the new owner.
