Franchise or independent? What SBA loan outcomes show

SBA 7(a) loans · FY2010-2019 and FY2020 to June 2026 · data as of June 30, 2026

Franchises are often pitched as the safer way to own a business. SBA's loan-level data lets us check one piece of that: every 7(a) loan to a franchise carries a franchise code. Here's how franchise and independent borrowers compare.

What it shows

  • Across all FY2010-2019 7(a) loans, franchise loans were charged off more often by count (8.7% vs 7.0%), and the dollar loss rates were close (3.1% vs 2.9%). The data doesn't show franchises as safer overall.
  • Within the same industry the picture is mixed: franchises had the lower charge-off rate in 11 of 22 industries. They did better in gas stations, child care, hardware stores and barber shops, and worse in full-service restaurants (2012 code), snack bars, auto repair and janitorial.
  • Franchise loans were much larger: median $287,500 vs $100,400 in FY2010-2019. Our inference: that likely reflects franchise borrowers financing build-out, equipment and franchise fees together, though the data doesn't show what each loan paid for.
  • For business purchases, franchise and independent acquisition loans had similar charge-off rates in FY2010-2019 (4.6% vs 4.5%), but that sample is small.
  • Our inference, not a finding: the industry and the deal seem to matter more than whether there's a brand on the sign. In gyms, and in restaurants under the current NAICS codes, franchises did not outperform independents (under the older 2007 restaurant codes they did somewhat better).

How we calculated this

  • Franchise = the loan's FranchiseCode field is filled in; independent = blank. We didn't break out individual brands: the same brand often appears under several codes and spellings.
  • Loans counted: every loan in the file for that group, excluding cancelled, undisbursed ("COMMIT") and not-funded loans.
  • Charge-off rate (count) = loans with status "Charged Off" ÷ loans counted. Charge-off $ rate = total gross charge-off amount ÷ total gross approved amount.
  • SBA withholds the status of loans that are still active (shown as "EXEMPT" in the file), so a loan that hasn't charged off yet counts as not charged off. Groups with more withheld loans may see their rates rise.
